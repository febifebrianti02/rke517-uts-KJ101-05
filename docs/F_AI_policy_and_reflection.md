# F. My AI policy and reflection

## My five rules for using AI in health information work
1. Rule: I never paste real patient data (names, RM numbers, diagnoses) into an AI tool; I use fictional or removed-identity data only. Reason: health data is personal data that the clinic must protect, and I cannot control where the text goes after I send it (see UU No. 27 Tahun 2022 on personal data protection).
2. Rule: I use only the facts from the client's files; everything else the AI adds is labelled "(my assumption)" or written as an open question. Reason: AI fills gaps with things that sound right, and in this case it suggested things such as who approves a referral that the client never said.
3. Rule: when two sources disagree, the AI may point it out but I do not let it choose. I ask the client. Reason: the rule belongs to the clinic. Here the form (SPO RM-51) says print after the doctor signs and the voice note says before; a wrong guess could change a clinic rule without anyone deciding it.
4. Rule: I never let AI make or write a clinical decision, such as a diagnosis or a choice of hospital. Reason: a doctor is accountable for the referral, and a wrong word in a referral letter can harm a patient.
5. Rule: I write down every important AI use, and I submit only work that I can explain myself. Reason: I am accountable for every line, the oral check asks me to explain it, and a log shows my reader what I checked and where the AI was wrong.

## Reflection (about one page)
- **Where AI helped most:** AI helped most with structure and speed. It gave me the order of the work, a table that put the four sources side by side, and the first draft of the requirements, the PRD and the diagrams. The table made the print-order contradiction easy to see: the form and the interview say print after signing, the voice note says before, and Mas Rizky's description of the work says before. It also noticed that the staff chat contains a data item (the hospital reply letter) that the form and the interview never mention, so it became a data store and an entity.
- **Where AI was wrong for my case:** AI could not listen to the voice note, which is the file with the key contradiction, so the text had to come from me. It also labelled its own guesses as proposals, but some of them lean to one side. Its compromise for print order follows the SPO, while the client's latest message asks for the opposite, so the decision must go to the client. It also made small mistakes: spreadsheet IDs that did not match Part A, a wrong count of the steps in the interview, and notes on top of arrows in the flowmap. I found these by comparing the files with each other and with the case files.
